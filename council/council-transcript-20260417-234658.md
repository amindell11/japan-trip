## Chairman Synthesis

### Where the Council Agrees

Five advisors converged independently on three points:

1. **Sakura Cross Shinjuku's "1 room, 2 double beds for 4" is a bed-config problem, not a win.** Two straight male friends sharing a Japanese double bed (140cm) is an unresolved assumption. Every advisor except D flagged it. The spreadsheet scored the hotel 91 without ever asking who sleeps with whom.
2. **Akihabara is the wrong base for the Tokyo arrival leg.** Day 3 (Mt Takao via Keio Line) and Day 4 (Kawaguchi bus) both depart from Shinjuku. Two cross-Tokyo commutes with hiking packs in rainy season is a cost the distance-score never captured.
3. **Eslead Namba South III wins Osaka.** Namba is the correct transit node for Dotonbori + USJ + Osaka Castle. Shinsekai is a place to visit, not sleep.

### Where the Council Clashes

1. **Ryokan vs business hotel for Tokyo arrival.** Expansionist (D) wants Onyado Nono Asakusa for the ryokan onsen "core memory" experience ($1,280 trip). Everyone else wants Sakura Cross Shinjuku ($440) for transit logic. The honest read: D is optimizing for travel-blog memory, not the user's stated priority ("price and closeness over luxury"). Reasonable advisors disagree because D is right that $220/person on a once-in-a-lifetime ryokan in Asakusa is a defensible splurge — but it violates the stated budget premise and the Day 3/4 transit reality.

2. **Kyoto: Aoi Nakamoto guesthouse vs APA Gion hotel.** D calls APA "a business hotel you could get in Cleveland" and wants the authentic tatami experience. The Contrarian, First Principles Thinker, and Executor all note that shared bath + futons-after-hiking-and-camping in rainy season is real friction, and that a couple sharing a bathhouse with strangers is unflagged friction. APA wins on the stated priorities, but if the group is up for some discomfort in exchange for authenticity, Aoi is defensible.

### Blind Spots the Council Caught

Three things only surfaced in the peer review round:

1. **The June 10–12 calendar gap is unaddressed.** Kyoto ends Jun 10, Osaka starts Jun 12 — that's 2 unaccounted nights. Presumably Kasagi + Hiyoshi/GRAX camping, but no response priced a fallback if camping is rained out during tsuyu season. Contrarian half-flagged it; no one solved it.

2. **Luggage logistics across camping nights.** Four people, two camping nights in Tokyo (Kawaguchi) and Kyoto (Kasagi/Hiyoshi). Where do suitcases live? **Takkyubin / takuhaibin** (luggage forwarding, ~¥2,000/bag) from Tokyo-arrival hotel → Kyoto hotel, and Kyoto-hotel → Osaka-hotel, sidesteps the issue entirely. This one detail dissolves half the neighborhood debate — if you're not carrying camping gear + luggage to the bus, Shinjuku vs Akihabara matters less.

3. **Four-adult-per-room legality.** Many Japanese hotels legally cap rooms at 2–3 guests. The Rakuten API returned availability, but the user should confirm the specific room config (some hotels add surcharges or require a second room for the 4th person).

Unconfirmed but important: **Day 15 departure airport** (Narita or Haneda) — determines whether the Tokyo-return hotel near Akihabara is actually convenient.
